Privacy policy

Effective 19 November 2026

In short: Sorced has no accounts of its own and doesn't track you. Your progress stays on your iPhone. Seal I is free; the full game is a one-time purchase handled by Apple. Multiplayer is free and uses Apple's Game Center; players without the full game see ads between Multiplayer rounds, served by Google AdMob (see Multiplayer and ads). The game sends anonymous play stats and crash reports that help me improve it, and outside Europe it tells Meta about installs, duels cleared and purchases, so I can see whether my Facebook and Instagram ads work. You can turn all of that off at any time in Settings. This website counts visits without cookies (see Website statistics).

Who makes Sorced

Sorced is made by Ruben Depover, an independent developer, who is responsible for the data described here. You can reach me at ruben.depover@gmail.com.

What stays on your device

Your save game (progress, stars, unlocks, settings) is stored only on your iPhone. It isn't uploaded anywhere, and deleting the app deletes it.

What is sent

Unless you turn off Share play stats in Sorced's Settings, the game sends:

  • Gameplay events: which screens you open, which duels you start and how they end (score, stars, duration, the runes and spells you drew, how often a stroke wasn't recognised), seals and duels you complete, Gauntlet runs, upgrades, and changes to settings.
  • Purchase events: when the full-game offer is shown, and whether a purchase or restore was started, completed, cancelled or failed, with its price, currency, App Store country and Apple's transaction number. Payment itself is handled entirely by Apple: I never see your card or your Apple Account.
  • Crash reports: what the app was doing when it crashed.
  • Device details: device model, iOS version, app version and language.
  • A random ID created for this installation, so events from the same game can be counted together. It isn't linked to your name, your Apple ID or any other app.

Meta (Facebook and Instagram ads)

To measure whether my ads on Facebook and Instagram lead to people playing and buying Sorced, the App Store version of the game uses Meta's SDK, but only when all of these hold: Share play stats is on, and neither your App Store country nor your device region is in the EU, the EEA, the UK or Switzerland. It then sends Meta: that the app was installed and opened, when you clear a duel for the first time (and when you beat Seal I), and App Store purchases with their price and currency, together with device details such as model, iOS version, app version and language. Meta receives your IP address with these events.

Sorced never shows the tracking prompt and never reads your device's advertising identifier, so Meta can't link these events to you across other apps and websites; Apple's privacy-preserving install reports (SKAdNetwork) tell Meta which ad led to an install. Meta processes the data under its own privacy policy, possibly in the United States.

Multiplayer and ads

Game Center. Multiplayer finds opponents through Apple's Game Center, with the Game Center account you are signed in to on your device. Your opponent sees your Game Center nickname, your ladder rating and the duelist you chose; the two phones exchange the strokes you draw during the duel, directly or through Apple's servers. Your rating is posted to the Sorced leaderboard in Game Center. Apple handles this under its own privacy policy; I don't receive your Game Center ID or nickname.

Ads. If you don't own the full game, Multiplayer shows a full-screen ad between some rounds (never during a duel, and none in your first matches). The ads come from Google AdMob. To show and measure them, Google's SDK receives your device's IP address (from which a rough location such as the country is derived), device and app details, and how you interact with the ad. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland the game first asks for your consent with Google's consent form; you can say no, and you then get only non-personalised, limited ads. Sorced never shows Apple's tracking prompt, so Google can't read your device's advertising identifier. Google processes this data under its own privacy policy. Buying the full game removes the ads.

Website statistics

This website counts visits in a privacy-friendly way, with the same analytics service as the game (PostHog, EU). It sets no cookies and stores nothing on your device, so it needs no cookie banner.

For each page you open it sends: the page address (without anything after the “?” except campaign tags such as utm_source, and a yes/no for “came from a Facebook or Instagram ad”; Meta's own click code, fbclid, is never sent), the website you came from (its name only), whether you use a phone or a computer and your screen width, whether you tapped the “Download on the App Store” button, and, if you visit with an Android device, that an Android visitor saw the page. It also sends your browser's technical description (its “user agent”, such as “iPhone, Safari 18”) and your time zone. Your IP address reaches PostHog's servers as with any request; PostHog uses it, together with the user agent and time zone, only to make an anonymous code that changes every day (so a visitor is counted once per day), and then discards the IP address. That code can't be used to identify you, and nothing links your visits on different days or to the game.

The App Store buttons carry a campaign tag (for example “meta” or “site”) so that Apple's anonymous App Store reports can tell me how many downloads came through this website.

If your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, the website sends nothing at all. The website has no advertising or social-media trackers (no Meta Pixel, no Google Analytics). The legal basis is my legitimate interest in knowing how the website is used and whether my ads bring visitors.

What is never collected

No name, email address or account; no contacts, photos or precise location; no advertising identifier; and no tracking across other apps or websites. (Google AdMob, which shows Multiplayer ads, receives the IP address as described under Multiplayer and ads.) PostHog discards your IP address when the data arrives (for the website, after making the daily code described above), and no location is derived from it.

Who processes it

The stats are stored with PostHog, an analytics service, in its EU data centre, on my behalf and only for the purposes below. Outside Europe, the events listed under Meta also go to Meta. Multiplayer ads involve Google, and matches Apple's Game Center, as described above. The data isn't sold or shared with anyone else.

Why

To find duels that are too hard or too easy, strokes the game misreads, and crashes, so they can be fixed; to see whether players who reach the end of Seal I buy the full game; and to measure whether my ads bring players. The legal basis is my legitimate interest in keeping the game fair, stable and fun. The data is anonymous and limited to what that needs, and you can object at any time by turning sharing off.

How long it's kept

Stats, from the game and from this website, are kept for 12 months, then deleted.

Your choices

  • Stop sharing: turn off Share play stats in Sorced's Settings. Nothing more is sent to PostHog, and Meta's SDK no longer starts from the next launch.
  • Ads: in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, change or withdraw your ad consent at any time with Ad privacy choices in Sorced's Settings. Anywhere, buying the full game removes the ads.
  • Delete what was sent: write to the address above with the Stats ID shown in Settings, and I'll delete the data linked to it.
  • You can also ask what data is held about that ID, or complain to your data protection authority.

Children

Sorced isn't aimed at children under 13, and the stats contain nothing that identifies a person.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date.
